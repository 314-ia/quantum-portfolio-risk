Quantum portfolio risk with Qiskit

A parametric quantum circuit that estimates the risk of each asset in an investment portfolio, built from real market prices and run in Qiskit and on an IBM quantum computer. An introductory notebook and slide deck, written in Spanish.

How the method works

01

One qubit per asset

Twelve well-known stocks, from Coca-Cola to Tesla and MercadoLibre, become twelve qubits.

02

RY loads the asset's own risk

A rotation by 2·arcsin(√R) leaves in each qubit the risk the asset carries on its own.

03

CRY couples correlated assets

Controlled gates connect the assets that move together, with a correlation of 0.6 or more. We read it as contagion.

04

A single measurement run

The frequency of 1 in each qubit is its risk index. The portfolio takes the six lowest.

What sets the method apart: the angles come from a formula, not from an optimizer. There is no loop of many executions as in VQE or QAOA, and one qubit per asset makes it possible to reach 110 assets on a current processor.

What can and cannot be claimed

  • The circuit runs on current hardware. With 12 qubits the real result almost matches the ideal one. With 110 qubits the noise grows and the overall risk ordering holds.
  • There is no quantum advantage. These circuits can be simulated on a laptop, and a classical method such as Markowitz's achieves lower-risk portfolios. With the same weighting rule, a trivial selection, the 6 assets with the lowest historical volatility, beats the circuit's selection. The weighting rule was changed after a first evaluation; the choice holds on the training data alone, but it should be said.
  • The value is in the validation method. The circuit is compared against its ideal version and against a classical model, and a possible route to an algorithmic advantage is to use it inside quantum amplitude estimation, which in theory needs fewer samples.

What the material includes

Qiskit notebook

Eight sections, from Yahoo Finance prices and the Markowitz efficient frontier to VQE, the risk circuit and the final evaluation. It runs on simulators in about a minute.

Slides

Seventeen slides plus a ten-slide appendix on the notebook: modern portfolio theory, parametric circuits, VQE and QAOA, the method, why it does not start with Hadamard gates, and how to read the results.

How to run it

git clone https://github.com/314-ia/quantum-portfolio-risk.git
cd quantum-portfolio-risk/CACIC
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r notebook/requirements.txt
.venv/bin/jupyter lab notebook/Finanzas_Cuanticas_CACIC2026.ipynb

The pinned versions require Python 3.12 or newer. Everything quantum runs on simulators, except one optional step that sends the circuit to a real IBM computer with a free account. Prices are downloaded from Yahoo Finance at run time.
